Self-taught: every course taken online.

I work between engineering and art direction. That means shipping production-grade interfaces in React and Node, then obsessing over the 200 milliseconds that make them feel alive — the easing on a panel, the weight of a headline, the moment a form confirms instead of reloads.

My process is simple and slightly stubborn: understand the real problem, reduce it to a clear system, prototype the risky parts early, and build the rest so it stays maintainable long after launch. No decoration without purpose.

Off-screen I'm a photographer: I shoot brand and editorial work and carry the image direction all the way into the interface, which is why the builds I ship tend to look art-directed rather than assembled. Nothing here came from a classroom. Every course behind this work was taken online — Coursera, Udemy, Google, Microsoft Azure and freeCodeCamp — self-taught, and still learning in public.

Case study 01 — Voice AI companion

FRIENDO

A voice-first AI companion: press the circle, talk, and it answers out loud — in real time, with a memory that survives the session.

Year
2026
Role
Solo product design & build
Timeline
Ongoing this year
Stack
Gemini API, realtime audio, local database

Problem

Voice assistants are usually text assistants with a microphone bolted on. You finish a sentence, wait for a spinner, and the reply arrives as a paragraph read aloud — and the next session starts from zero. That rhythm kills the thing that makes a conversation worth having: timing. A companion that forgets every plan you mentioned is not a companion; it is a search box with a personality.

Solution

Friendo streams audio in both directions at once, so the answer starts arriving while you are still sitting there. Barge-in lets you speak over it and it stops mid-sentence to listen. The screen holds one breathing circle that reacts to your voice — pure black, pixel type, nothing competing. Thirty seconds of silence is treated as absence rather than an error: it asks whether you are still there, and if you stay away it talks to itself the way a person alone in a room does, then tries again. Photos are answered only when you send one — no camera, no live screen feed.

Memory

Each session feeds a memory list: facts saved mid-conversation, plus details mined from the transcript that you never thought to announce. The list can be read, edited and deleted, and the next session starts where the last one stopped. Spoken turns are written down from the transcript the model actually produced, so the written history is the conversation itself rather than a second text pipeline.

The teacher

One companion is a Spanish teacher built for a single student. It loads your own notes before the first lesson, so it teaches from your real level instead of guessing. New words arrive three to five at a time, each in three passes — say it, break it into sounds, say it again, then give the meaning — with drills that return later. Corrections are immediate and kind: a wrong answer never passes, and it is never mocked. Learned words are saved as the syllabus, so nothing is taught twice, and the beginner mistakes in those old notes are corrected out loud and healed in the list. More than one character lives in the app, each with its own voice, accent colour, memories and conversations.

Outcome

Shipped as a private app by design: no account, no ads, no analytics; the Gemini key and the local database stay on the device; audio and any picture travel only to Google's Gemini API while in use. On Android it asks for the microphone and nothing else.

Case study 02 — Personal finance, offline

MONEXA

A private, offline budgeting and work-tracking app — no account, no ads, no internet required.

Year
2026
Role
Solo product design & build
Timeline
Ongoing this year
Stack
Local-first app, offline storage, Arabic/English RTL

Problem

Budgeting apps assume a salary, a cloud account and a stable month. Variable work — an 8-hour day here, a 12-hour day there, a day off you only decided on that morning — turns income into something you reconstruct rather than look up. Add bills that get paid in parts, savings moved in and out, and money owed in both directions, and the honest question goes unanswered: how much of this month is actually left? Most apps also want that answer stored on someone else's server.

Work log

A calendar heatmap replaces the timesheet. Each day is logged as 8 or 12 hours, and a long-press marks a day off in orange — because days off move from week to week. Monthly totals for 12-hour days, 8-hour days, absences and days off are calculated automatically, so the month's shape is visible at a glance instead of rebuilt in a spreadsheet.

Money

Income sits at the top and the remaining balance updates instantly. Fixed bills and recurring items accept partial payments; expenses carry a short description; savings track deposits and withdrawals with a net figure; debts and loans cover what is owed and what is owed to you, also partial. One live monthly summary — income, spent, saved, remaining — ties the two halves of the app together.

Privacy & portability

There are no servers, no tracking and no ads: the database lives on the phone and the financial data leaves only when the owner exports it. A month can go out as a clean PNG report; the full dataset goes out as JSON and restores in one step; a reset wipes everything. The interface runs in Arabic and English with proper RTL/LTR layout, in a dark palette built for nightly use, with an optional reminder at a chosen time to log the day.

2 languagesArabic and English with full RTL
